🤖 MARIO AI LAB

A deep Q-learning agent that learned to play the first level of Super Mario World from raw pixels — bundled into this app from the mario-ai research project (see ai/LICENSE).

HOW IT LEARNS

  • Watches the game as 32×32 + 64×64 grayscale screenshots, 4 frames of history, every 5th frame.
  • Stores what it sees in a 250k-entry replay memory, then trains on random batches.
  • Epsilon-greedy policy: random moves 80% of the time at first, down to 10% by action 400k.
  • ~6.6M parameters across three branches: action history, screenshot history, current screenshot + spatial transformer.

TRY THE AUTOPILOT DEMO

No GPU needed: a screen-reactive bot that watches whatever you load, like the DQN does — it taps through menus while the picture is still, follows the scroll direction once the world moves — horizontal and vertical, so it climbs and descends with the camera instead of only pushing left/right — holds run while the pixels demand it, and alternates A/B/X on the beat — jump in one game is attack in another — holds Y for run/dash, and works the L/R shoulders every other beat. Pause, game over, or a black fade goes quiet and it finds its own way back. One brain, every game. It stays on until you toggle it off — your own button presses just join in alongside it. It drives players 1 and 2 out of the box as two independent copilot engines — same screen, but each with its own timers, motion history, steering vote, menu guard, and release path, so player 2 never just parrots player 1 and one pilot seeking or releasing never stops the other. On character-select grids it walks the cursor through different rows and columns before confirming (instead of always taking the top-left cell), and both players press START, so P2 joins instead of idling. MIX alternates ~2.4s of platformer movement/hops with ~2.4s of UMK3 strikes/block/specials, so a game that changes genre between screens gets both. Narrow it in TUNE below. The trained Torch model itself needs CUDA + lsnes — see the project README.

Tune it below (thresholds, tap rate, seek confirm fallback) or flip the HUD toggle in the footer to watch its mind work: SEEK · d=2.1 vs PLAY · d=23.4 ▶. Button map: dirR=7 dirL=6 run=1 actions=[8,0,9] shoulders=[10,11] select=2 start=3.

FIGHTER PROFILE: UMK3

From the manual: RUN = hold R + Forward to close distance. BLOCK = hold L. Crouch kick = DOWN + X/A, uppercut = DOWN + Y, spin moves = hold AWAY + kick, jump-ins = UP(+toward) + punch timed on the way down. Fighter select takes any button except START; START picks menus. The UMK3 profile plays exactly this way — approach with RUN, strike flurries (button sequences land kombos), BLOCK phases, crouch uppercuts, jump-ins, and rotating special-motion strings — real ones from the booklet (Kano's knife, Liu Kang's fireball, Scorpion's spear, Sub-Zero's freeze…) — that trigger genuine specials across the roster. Sides alternate since facing is unknown.
