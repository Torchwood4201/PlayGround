It all started in 1891, when Dr. James Naismith noed up a bushel basket on a gym wall. Kids 
with nothing better to do on a winter afternoon threw a ball at it. Every once in a while the ball 
went in.. 50 began the game we call basketball. 


There were no pros in the early days, Nobody knew about lay ups, or dribbling, or no-look 
posses. If any one back then even dreamed about a Get-outta-the-wayin'-I-ain't-playin'- 
backboard-swayin'-game-delayin'-in-your-face-discrace-Monster Jam, he didn’t tell anybody

Every year great teams from all over the country battle for the chance to compete for the NBA 
World Championship. The season schedule is grueling—over 80 games. Some of the league's 
best players don't last to the end, succumbing to injuries or fatigue which results in poor play. 
And the coaches are no less vulnerable. Mos! seasons at least one head coach loses his job. 
Trades are made; deals are cut; starting lineups can change like the weather. Everyone from 
the franchise owners to the ball boys are butching it out to make the first cul. 


lt was no surprise that the handful of teams who earned a place in the 1993 Playoffs were 
burning with a hunger to win. Playoff series evolved into battles. The stars stepped up their 
play. Fans came out in full force to witness the greatest moments of the season. When it was all 
said ond done, two-time defending NBA World Champion Chicago Bulls faced off with the 
Season MVP Charles Barkely-powered Phoenix Suns, who sported the league's best record, for 
the crown of the NBA.

Shot-Clock 


In NBA basketball you have 24 seconds from the time you bring the ball in bounds to get a shot 
off, If the boll does not strike or clear the rim within 24 seconds, the referee calls a shot clock 
violation and the other team gets possession of the ball. 


You'll hear one of your fellow players yell “Shoot it!” or "Put it up!” when the shot clock runs 
down to five seconds. 


The shot-clock that keeps track of how many seconds you have left to shoo! appears at the 
bottom center of the screen. When there are less than 24 seconds left in the half, the shot clock 
shows the time remaining in the half.

Streaks, Slumps and Fatigue 


All players have their on days and their off days. The cagers in NBA SHOWDOWN are no 
exception. Sometimes a player gets hot. It seems every shot he puts up drops. Other days the 
same player couldn't make on unguarded layup. Streaks and Slumps usually don't last more 
than seven games, and you can bet Ron Barr and K.C. Darty will let you know if a player has 
been exceptionally hot or cold. It’s your job to make sure the hot player gets a lot of court time 
and the cool player rides it out on the pine or takes fewer shots. 


Fatigue can affect both a player and a team. If a player has been logging a lot of court time, or 
if he has been injured (INJURIES ON mode only), he can experience fatigue. This fatigue often 
leads to the player's HEALTH rating to diminish faster than it normally would; sometimes it 
leads to additional injuries. Make sure you rest even your best players now ond then. 

Team fatigue occurs after a team plays over its head for about five games. Coming off a big 
winning streak, a team is certainly going to experience o letdown, And the players’ bodies have 
to be tired ond beat up. If your team rises to the occasion and beats better teams or absolutely 
crushes teams of equal caliber, expect a toll to be taken sometime within the next few games,
